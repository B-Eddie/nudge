Almost all of them sell the same three things: something cute that **reacts to typing**, **collect hats or skins**, and **a timer or reminder**.
A visitor can't tell them apart from a screenshot, and none of them has a premise.

## Where Nudge is different
1. **A premise, not a mascot.** Four lazy prodigies who believe rest is a martial art. Each has a name, a voice and a Do-Nothing Expansion: Mika, Goro, Yuzu and Hasami.
2. **The body is the timer.** Nobody reads a countdown. You can see the bamboo, the yuzu tower, the cat's pupils and the crab's claw from across the room.
3. **Rest is the power source.** A full Expansion needs a rested pet; a tired one can only throw a small tantrum. That makes taking a break the way you "level up", without streaks or guilt.
4. **Mad at it for you.** The pet takes your side against the task you're avoiding, then offers to start it with you for ten minutes. No competitor is adversarial on your behalf.
5. **Manners.** Nudges wait for a pause in typing, hold during calls and let videos finish. Locking the Mac counts as a break.

What we don't compete on: number of pets, hats, AI chat, or watching AI agents.

## For reels
Lead with a character and a moment, not a feature list:
- The fizzle: a tired Hasami tries his Expansion, gets a tantrum, and is told to rest first.
- An Expansion over a tax form or Canvas, then "10 minutes, together".
- A timelapse of the bamboo being eaten down across a two-hour stretch.
- The cast reacting to the same tab (forty tabs, YouTube at 2am), each in its own voice.
Keep the four names and epithets consistent everywhere; they're the brand.

## IP note
The cast, art, names and technique names are original. The technique names are puns on a genre, not on any one work's characters. Keep it that way: no lookalike characters, logos or exact quotes.
